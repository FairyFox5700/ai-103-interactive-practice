1. Azure AI Content Safety — Overview & Access Points

Azure AI Content Safety is the AI service that powers guardrails — safety and security controls that detect harmful user- and AI-generated content across models, agents, and services. A guardrail control specifies what risk to detect, where to scan for it (input/output/tool response), and how the system should respond when found.

Two ways to use it

Access pointWhen to use
Guardrails in Microsoft FoundryYour models/agents are hosted in Foundry; you want built-in moderation during model/agent calls; first 1,000 text characters are moderated free.
Azure AI Content Safety Studio / standalone APIsModels/agents run outside Foundry or span multiple platforms; you need a consistent guardrail experience everywhere; you need moderation on all characters (standard Content Safety pricing applies); you need moderation beyond model/agent calls (e.g., user-generated content, marketplaces, chat).
Both access points are powered by the same underlying AI service — the difference is where and how you consume it, not the detection models themselves.

Where it's used

  • Moderating prompts submitted to a generative AI service and content produced by generative AI models
  • Online marketplaces, gaming chat/UGC, social platforms, enterprise media, K-12 filtering

Important exam fact: Azure AI Content Safety cannot be used to detect illegal child exploitation images — that requires specialized services (e.g., PhotoDNA), not Content Safety.

Content Safety Studio

A web portal (contentsafety.cognitive.azure.com) with: Moderate Text Content tool, Moderate Image Content tool, Monitor Online Activity dashboard, and Microsoft's built-in terms blocklists plus the ability to upload custom blocklists. Lets you test sensitivity levels and export code.

Deprecation watch (retiring April 30, 2027): Analyze Multimodal (preview), Custom categories (rapid) text+image, Connected/Disconnected Content Safety containers, Embedded Content Safety SDK, and Groundedness detection in Content Safety/Azure OpenAI are all public-preview capabilities slated for retirement — check the linked migration guidance pages if this appears on an exam scenario about long-term architecture choices.
Must know: Guardrails = the control layer; Azure AI Content Safety = the underlying detection service. The same models power Foundry's built-in guardrails and the standalone Content Safety resource — choose standalone when you need full-character moderation, multi-platform consistency, or moderation outside of model/agent calls (e.g., UGC on a website).

2. Harm Categories & Severity Levels

Content Safety classifies harmful content into four core harm categories, applied consistently to both text and image content using neural multiclass classification models:

CategoryCovers
Hate and FairnessDiscriminatory language tied to race, ethnicity, nationality, gender identity, sexual orientation, religion, appearance, disability, harassment/bullying.
SexualAnatomical/sexual language, romantic/sexual acts, nudity, pornography, abuse, child exploitation/grooming.
ViolencePhysical harm, weapons/guns, bullying/intimidation, terrorism/extremism, stalking.
Self-HarmSelf-injury, suicide, eating disorders, related bullying.

A fifth category, Task Adherence, applies specifically to AI agents — it flags when tool use/response is misaligned, unintended, or premature relative to the user's instructions (not a "harm" category in the traditional sense, but part of the guardrails family).

Severity scale

  • Text: full 0–7 scale (or trimmed to 0/2/4/6).
  • Image: trimmed scale only — 0, 2, 4, 6.
  • Image with text (multimodal): full 0–7 scale (or trimmed).

For Guardrails configuration (Foundry), severities roll up into four human-readable levels:

Severity levelDescription
SafeNo harmful material; always annotated, never filtered/blocked, not configurable.
LowMild: prejudiced views, mild fictional depictions, personal experience retellings.
MediumModerate: graphic depictions, bullying, content promoting harmful acts.
HighSevere: extremist content, explicit depictions, content endorsing serious harm.

Blocking level vs. severity (guardrail configuration)

Blocking levelBehavior
OffNothing blocked for this category (may still annotate). Requires approval for modified guardrails on output.
LowBlocks only high-severity content — least restrictive.
MediumBlocks medium + high severity.
HighBlocks low + medium + high — most restrictive.
Watch the naming trap: a blocking level of "Low" blocks only high-severity content (least restrictive), while a blocking level of "High" blocks everything from low severity up (most restrictive). The blocking level name describes the threshold strictness, not which severities pass through untouched.

Classifiers are trained/tested primarily on Chinese, English, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Portuguese, and Spanish; other languages are supported but with variable accuracy — always evaluate false positives/negatives for your target languages.

Must know: There are exactly 4 core harm categories — Hate & Fairness, Sexual, Violence, Self-Harm — each scored across text and image on a severity scale (0–7 for text, trimmed 0/2/4/6 for image). A "Safe" classification is never a factual claim; it just means the classifier found no higher severity for that category — it's not proof of accuracy, policy compliance, or complete safety.

3. Text vs. Image Moderation APIs

APIPurpose
Analyze TextScans text for sexual, violence, hate, and self-harm content with multi-severity levels.
Analyze ImageScans images for the same four harm categories with multi-severity levels.

Both are "Content analysis" APIs, distinct from the "AI safety and prompt protection" family (Prompt Shields, Groundedness, Protected Material, Task Adherence) which targets generative-AI-specific risks rather than general UGC moderation.

Must know: Analyze Text and Analyze Image are separate REST endpoints, each independently scored against the same 4 harm categories — choose the endpoint matching the content modality; use the multimodal "Analyze Multimodal" (preview, retiring) only for combined image+text scenarios.

4. Prompt Shields — Jailbreak & Indirect Prompt Injection Detection

Prompt Shields (formerly Jailbreak risk detection) detect attempts to manipulate a model's behavior through adversarial inputs. This is a core AI-103 exam topic spanning multiple objective domains (guardrails + agent security).

Two attack types

Attack typeEntry pointDescription
User prompt attacks (direct jailbreak)userPrompt field (API) / user_prompts (Foundry)Malicious prompts trying to bypass system instructions or safety training — enabled by default in Foundry.
Document attacks (indirect prompt injection)documents fieldHidden instructions embedded in document content (documents, emails, web pages, retrieved RAG content) that try to hijack the model session. For agents, can also scan at the tool response intervention point (preview). Off by default in Foundry — must be explicitly enabled.

Subtypes of user prompt attacks

  • Attempt to change system rules — "ignore your instructions," unrestricted persona requests
  • Embedding a conversation mock-up — fake conversational turns crafted to confuse the model
  • Role-play — assigning the model an alternate persona without the original restrictions
  • Encoding attacks — ciphers, Base64/URL encoding, or stylistic tricks to sneak past filters

Subtypes of document attacks (indirect prompt injection)

  • Manipulated Content, Information Gathering, Availability, Fraud, Malware, Allowing a compromised LLM to access system infrastructure, plus the same "change system rules / conversation mock-up / role-play" classes as user-prompt attacks.
Classic exam distinction: a malicious instruction typed directly by the end user is a user prompt attack (direct). A malicious instruction hidden inside a retrieved document, email, or web page that the LLM ingests as context is a document attack — this is "indirect prompt injection," and it is scanned via the documents field, not userPrompt.

Response structure includes detected (true/false — was an attack pattern found) and filtered (true/false — was it actually blocked), which can differ depending on your blocking configuration. In Content Safety's standalone API, the service never blocks automatically — your application logic decides whether to allow, block, review, or escalate. In Foundry guardrails, blocking can be automatic based on your configured control.

Prompt Shields models are primarily trained/evaluated on English; multilingual support exists but accuracy can vary — validate with representative non-English attack samples if your app is multilingual.

Must know: Prompt Shields detect manipulation attempts only — they do not judge whether content is harmful, factually accurate, grounded, or policy-compliant (that's what harm categories / groundedness / protected-material detection are for). Also remember: document-attack (indirect injection) detection is off by default in Foundry guardrails and must be explicitly turned on — a very testable "gotcha."

5. Groundedness Detection & Protected Material Detection

FeaturePurposeStatus
Groundedness detectionDetects whether an LLM's text response is actually grounded in the source material the user supplied (catches hallucination / fabricated claims not supported by context).Preview; Content Safety/Azure OpenAI version is scheduled to retire April 30, 2027 — check for migration guidance.
Protected material text detectionScans AI-generated text for known copyrighted/protected content, e.g. song lyrics, articles, recipes, selected web content.GA feature.
Task Adherence APIDetects when an AI agent's tool invocations are misaligned, unintended, or premature relative to user intent — an agent-specific guardrail.Current.
Groundedness detection answers "is this answer supported by the provided documents?" — different from Prompt Shields (which answers "is this input trying to manipulate the model?") and from harm categories (which answer "is this content offensive/dangerous?"). Know which guardrail answers which question.
Must know: Groundedness detection targets hallucination/RAG-fidelity risk, not toxicity — pair it with harm-category filters and Prompt Shields for full-spectrum generative-AI guardrails coverage.

6. Custom Categories & Blocklists

FeatureDescription
Custom categories (standard) API (preview)Train your own custom content categories beyond the 4 built-in harm categories, then scan text for matches.
Custom categories (rapid) API (preview — retiring)Define emerging harmful-content patterns quickly (e.g., a new trend) and scan both text and images for matches without full model retraining.
BlocklistsExact-match term lists layered on top of the AI classifiers. Microsoft provides built-in profanity blocklists in Content Safety Studio; you can also upload your own custom terms specific to your domain/brand.
Blocklists are deterministic exact/fuzzy string matches — useful for known banned terms, brand names, or slurs that the neural classifier might score as "safe" in context. They complement, not replace, the AI harm-category classifiers.
Must know: Use blocklists for exact known terms (deterministic, fast, zero training); use custom categories when you need the AI classifier itself to learn a brand-new harmful pattern that doesn't map to the 4 built-in categories.

7. Content Safety vs. Azure OpenAI / Foundry Built-in Content Filters

AspectFoundry / Azure OpenAI built-in guardrailsStandalone Azure AI Content Safety resource
ScopeApplies automatically to prompts/completions flowing through a Foundry-hosted model or agent deployment.Callable from any application, any platform, independent of where your model runs.
CostFree moderation for the first 1,000 text characters per request.Billed per Content Safety pricing for all characters/images processed.
ConfigurationConfigured as guardrail "controls" per model deployment/agent (blocking level per category, enable/disable Prompt Shields document-attack scanning, etc.).Configured via direct API calls / Content Safety Studio; your app owns the allow/block/escalate decision logic.
Best forConsistent, low-friction protection for models/agents already inside Foundry.Cross-platform consistency, moderating non-model content (UGC, marketplaces), or needing full-character coverage beyond the free tier.
Must know: Both paths use the same underlying detection models — the exam-relevant distinction is architectural (where/how you invoke it and what's free vs. billed), not detection quality. Pick standalone Content Safety when protecting non-Foundry or multi-platform workloads, or moderating content that never touches a model call at all (e.g., raw user uploads).

8. Azure Translator — Overview & Service Family

Azure Translator (part of Foundry Tools, formerly "Azure AI services") is a cloud-based neural machine translation (NMT) service supporting 100+ languages and dialects, usable on any OS via REST API, SDKs, or the Foundry portal.

FeatureWhat it does
Text translation (2026-06-06, GA)Newest API version; adds model choice (NMT or LLM), adaptive custom translation, tone/gender controls.
Text translation v3 (GA)The long-standing stable API; supports dynamic dictionary and "prevent translation" markers.
Document translation (async/batch)Translate many/large files via Blob Storage, preserving structure/format.
Document translation (sync/single file)Translate one file directly in the request/response — no Blob Storage required.
Adaptive custom translationSupply 5–10,000 aligned source–target segment pairs to steer an LLM's translation style/terminology in minutes (no full model training).
Custom TranslatorTrain a fully custom domain-specific NMT model using your own parallel documents/dictionaries.
Use Microsoft Foundry portal for a no-code interface to text translation and synchronous document translation; use REST/SDK for production automation.

9. Text Translation API

Core operations

  • Languages — lists supported languages (no authentication required).
  • Translate — translates source text to one or more target languages in a single POST request.
  • Transliterate — converts text between writing systems/scripts (e.g., Cyrillic → Latin) for language pairs that need script conversion — distinct from translation (meaning) vs. transliteration (script/phonetics only).

Key capabilities (exam-relevant)

CapabilityDetail
Language auto-detectOmit the source language and the API identifies it automatically.
Profanity filteringv3 parameter to mark or omit profane terms in the translated output.
Dynamic dictionaryForce specific word/phrase translations inline per-request using markup, without training a custom model.
"Prevent translation"Mark spans of text (e.g., product/brand names) to pass through untranslated.
Model choice (NMT vs. LLM)New in 2026-06-06: choose standard NMT for speed/cost/consistency, or an LLM deployment (e.g., GPT-5.1) for more nuance — LLM-based translation requires a Microsoft Foundry resource.
Tone & gender controlsLLM-based translation only: select formal/informal/neutral tone and gender-specific phrasing.
Adaptive custom translation (per-request)Supply up to 5 reference translations or an adaptive dataset index ID to steer LLM output style/terminology without full custom-model training.

Authentication options

  • Resource key — Ocp-Apim-Subscription-Key header (simplest to start).
  • Time-limited token — short-lived bearer token in Authorization header.
  • Microsoft Entra ID — managed identities/service principals for keyless, enterprise-grade auth.
v3.0 is still GA, but the new 2026-06-06 API version introduces breaking changes (request array renamed inputs, response array renamed value). Thoroughly test before migrating production workloads from v3 to 2026-06-06.
Must know: Dynamic dictionary and "prevent translation" are per-request, markup-based controls — they require no model training. Contrast with Custom Translator, which trains a persistent, reusable domain model. LLM-based translation (tone/gender controls, model choice) requires a Microsoft Foundry resource, not just a plain Translator resource.

10. Document Translation

Document translation preserves the original layout/format/structure of complex files while translating their content. It supports two distinct workflows:

ModeUse caseRequires Blob Storage?
Asynchronous (batch)Translate multiple/large documents; process runs in the background.Yes — needs source and target containers in Azure Blob Storage, plus SAS tokens or managed identity for authorization.
Synchronous (single file)Translate exactly one document, response returned directly to the caller.No — requires only a Translator resource with a custom domain endpoint.

Key features

  • Preserves original layout/format across both modes
  • Applies general or custom translation models (Custom Translator)
  • Applies custom glossaries for terminology control
  • Automatic source-language detection, including documents with mixed-language content (batch mode)
  • (Batch only) Translate embedded image text within .jpeg/.png/.bmp/.webp files, and image text inside .docx/.pptx files, while preserving design/layout (billed per-image)
Exam trigger phrase: "translate many files from Blob storage preserving the folder structure" → asynchronous/batch document translation. "Translate a single uploaded file and get the result back immediately in the response" → synchronous document translation, no storage account needed.
Must know: Only the asynchronous/batch workflow requires an Azure Blob Storage account (source + target containers, SAS token or managed identity); the synchronous single-file workflow needs only a Translator resource with a custom domain endpoint.

11. Custom Translator & Adaptive Custom Translation

OptionHow it worksBest for
Custom TranslatorUpload parallel documents/dictionaries via the Custom Translator portal; trains a dedicated custom NMT model for your domain/terminology/style.Long-lived, high-volume domain-specific translation needs (legal, medical, manufacturing jargon) where consistent terminology matters over time.
Adaptive custom translationUpload 5–10,000 prealigned source–target segment pairs (≤500 chars each); builds a bilingual index in minutes to steer an LLM's output (e.g., GPT-5.1) via the Translator API.Fast, lightweight terminology/style adaptation without a full training cycle; works with the LLM translation path, not classic NMT.
Must know: Custom Translator = train a standalone NMT model (longer setup, reusable, classic workflow). Adaptive custom translation = quickly bias an LLM's translation output using a handful of example pairs (minutes, not a training job) — don't confuse the two on the exam.

12. When to Use Translator vs. an LLM/Foundry Model for Translation

ScenarioRecommended approach
High-volume, low-latency, consistent, cost-predictable translation at scale (e.g., product catalogs, UI strings, support tickets)Azure Translator (NMT) — purpose-built, cheaper per character, deterministic quality, mature SLAs.
Need tone control, paraphrase flexibility, context-aware idiomatic translation, or translation combined with summarization/rewriting in one callLLM-based translation (via Translator's 2026-06-06 model-choice option, or directly via a Foundry chat model) — more flexible, but higher cost/latency and less deterministic.
Need to preserve complex document formatting (Word, PowerPoint, PDF) at scaleDocument translation API (NMT-backed) — purpose-built for structure preservation; LLMs are not designed for this.
Need domain-specific terminology/style with light per-request customizationAdaptive custom translation (LLM-backed) or dynamic dictionary (NMT-backed) for quick wins; Custom Translator for a persistent trained model.
Translation is one step inside a broader agentic/RAG pipeline with reasoning or multi-turn contextLLM-powered translation flow in Foundry — easier to blend with other reasoning tasks in the same model call.
The AI-103 objective phrase "translate text by using Azure Translator in Foundry Tools or LLM-powered translation flows" is testing exactly this trade-off: NMT (Translator) = speed, cost, consistency, scale; LLM = flexibility, tone/nuance, integration with reasoning — know when each wins.
Must know: Azure Translator's NMT engine remains the default choice for consistency, cost, and scale; LLM-based translation (now selectable within the Translator API itself as of 2026-06-06, requiring a Foundry resource) trades some of that consistency for tone/gender control and contextual nuance — the exam wants you to match the scenario's priority (consistency vs. flexibility) to the right engine.
